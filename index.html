About

From math-heavy paths to building things

I moved to Busan to study business in English at Dong-A University. Before settling on Integrated Business Management, I seriously looked at AI engineering, data science, and investment banking. I'd rather compare options and rule things out deliberately than decide on impulse, and each of those three paths pointed toward a career built on math I didn't want to depend on. Business gave me a way to keep building — brands, ideas, eventually a company — without that constraint.

The long-term plan is to start my own brand, most likely in cosmetics, though a content-based brand or a gym-and-service business are still open possibilities. Before that, I want a few years in a marketing or business-adjacent role — not to delay the goal, but to leave it with capital and an actual sense of how a brand gets built, instead of starting blind. The Korean, the reading, and the training below aren't side projects; they're the discipline I'm betting the eventual brand on.

Interests

What I watch, read, and pay attention to

I'm drawn to intelligent, fast-paced films, especially ones built around business or finance. Watching someone read a room, spot an opening, or push past a limit teaches me almost as much as the books do — just in a different register.

  • The Wolf of Wall StreetAmbition without guardrails
  • The FounderBuilding something from nothing
  • The Big ShortReading the market when no one else will
  • MoneyballWinning with a better model
  • Catch Me If You CanSheer nerve and reinvention
  • Ford v FerrariObsessive craft under pressure
  • OppenheimerThe weight of consequential work
  • The TerminalPatience in an impossible situation

Outside of film, I follow AI, productivity tools, automation, and startups pretty closely — mostly because they're the tools I expect to actually rely on once I'm building something of my own. Klaus is the one exception on this list: an animated film, but it's stayed with me for how plainly it makes the case for genuine kindness, with no lesson dressed up as anything more than what it is.
